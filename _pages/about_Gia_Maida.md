### Gianna Maida (Master's student)

Gia, originally from Chicago, Illinois, earned her Bachelor of Science in Biology from Florida Gulf Coast University (FGCU) in 2025. Her professional experience spans a variety of environmental and conservation roles, including hunting operations with the Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission (FWC), environmental education at Everglades Wonder Gardens, and an internship with Charlotte County Natural Resources.

Gia’s research interests include habitat quality, wildlife ecology, population genetics, and environmental policy. As an undergraduate, her research focused primarily on semi-aquatic snakes, with an emphasis on the ecological factors influencing their distribution and capture rates. In 2026, she began pursuing her master’s degree at FGCU, where her research focuses on the population genetics of semi-aquatic snakes in Southwest Florida, incorporating data collected during her undergraduate research. Her current work investigates how habitat isolation and fragmentation influence genetic connectivity and population structure, with the goal of informing conservation and management strategies for wetland-dependent species.
